A balcony herb garden for under A$50

You don't need a raised bed or a big budget to grow herbs you'll actually use. Here's a simple balcony setup that comes in under fifty dollars, and how to keep it going through a Brisbane year.

Which herbs to start with in Brisbane

This is where a lot of generic gardening advice goes wrong for us. South-East Queensland is subtropical, so our "easy" season for many herbs is the cooler, drier half of the year, while summer suits heat lovers.

Warm season (roughly September to March)

  • Basil loves the heat. Pinch out the flower heads to keep it leafy. Thai basil is especially tough in our summers.
  • Chilli isn't technically a herb, but it earns a pot. Very productive in Brisbane.
  • Vietnamese mint and lemongrass handle heat and humidity far better than many Mediterranean herbs.

Cool season (roughly April to August)

  • Coriander bolts to seed quickly in hot weather, so sow it in autumn and winter for a much better harvest.
  • Parsley, dill and chives all do well through our mild winters.

Most of the year

  • Mint grows almost year-round but spreads aggressively. Always give it its own pot.
  • Rosemary and thyme will cope with sun but hate wet feet. Use a free-draining mix and don't overwater, especially in the wet season.

Setting it up

  1. Find the light. Most herbs want at least 4–6 hours of direct sun. In Brisbane, an east-facing spot with morning sun and afternoon shade is ideal in summer. A harsh west-facing balcony may need some shade cloth from December to February.
  2. Fill the pots with moist potting mix to about 2 cm below the rim.
  3. Plant seedlings at the same depth they were in their punnet, firm gently, and water in well.
  4. Mulch the surface lightly to slow evaporation.
  5. Harvest often. Regular picking from the tips keeps herbs bushy and productive.

Keeping costs down

  • Grow from seed where it's easy (basil, coriander, dill). One packet lasts several sowings. Sow into a fine seed raising mix rather than ordinary potting mix, which is too coarse for tiny seeds.
  • Take cuttings. Mint, basil and Vietnamese mint root readily in a glass of water.
  • Reuse potting mix for a second round by mixing in some fresh mix and a little compost, but start fresh if the previous plant had disease.
