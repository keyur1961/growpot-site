Five watering mistakes that kill balcony plants in Brisbane

It's easy to blame the heat when a potted plant dies in Brisbane. More often, the problem is water: too much, too little, or at the wrong time. Here are the mistakes I see most, and how to avoid them.

1. Watering on a schedule instead of checking

Watering "every morning" sounds responsible, but plants don't use water on a timetable. A pot in full sun in January might need water daily. The same pot in a cloudy week in July might need it once a week. Constantly wet mix starves roots of oxygen and leads to root rot, which looks, confusingly, like wilting from thirst.

Fix: use the finger test. Push a finger 2–3 cm into the mix. If it's dry at that depth, water. If it's still damp, wait. Lifting the pot works too: a dry pot is noticeably lighter.

2. Leaving water sitting in saucers

Saucers protect your balcony, but a saucer that stays full keeps the bottom of the pot waterlogged. In Brisbane there's a second problem: standing water is ideal mosquito breeding habitat, and mosquitoes can breed in it within about a week in warm weather.

Fix: empty saucers 20–30 minutes after watering, and after rain. Pot feet that lift the pot slightly above the saucer help too.

3. Light sprinkles instead of a proper soak

A quick splash wets the top few centimetres and runs off, leaving the root zone dry. Roots then stay near the surface, where they're most exposed to heat.

Fix: water slowly until it runs out of the drainage holes, then stop. If water rushes straight through without soaking in, the mix may have dried out and become water-repellent. Stand the pot in a bucket for 20 minutes to rewet it.

4. Watering in the heat of the afternoon

Watering at 2 pm on a 34°C day isn't harmful in itself, and a wilting plant should be watered whenever you notice it. But as a habit, midday watering loses more to evaporation, and evening watering in humid weather keeps leaves wet overnight, which encourages fungal problems like powdery mildew.

Fix: water in the morning when you can, and aim at the mix rather than the leaves.

5. Using a harsh jet on seedlings

A strong blast from a hose or tap can wash young seedlings out, splash mix onto leaves (spreading disease) and dig holes in the surface of the pot.

Fix: use a watering can with a rose (sprinkler head), or a gentle shower setting on a hose nozzle.

Product mention: a watering can with a fine rose, or an adjustable hose nozzle with a gentle shower setting.

Adjusting through the Brisbane year

  • Summer (Dec–Feb): hottest and wettest. Check pots daily, but after storms, check before watering, since many pots won't need anything for days. Make sure nothing is sitting in water.
  • Autumn (Mar–May): things ease off. Watering frequency usually drops.
  • Winter (Jun–Aug): dry but mild, with lower evaporation. Many pots only need water every few days. Overwatering is the main risk.
  • Spring (Sep–Nov): often dry and increasingly hot, with strong winds at times. This is when pots start drying out quickly again, often before the summer rain arrives.
